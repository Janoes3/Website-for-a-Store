Likert Scales 











Use Top‑2 / Bottom‑2 analysis (EXAM‑FRIENDLY)
This is straight from your DPDD slides.

Top‑2 = Positive experience (Agree + Strongly Agree)
Bottom‑2 = Negative experience (Disagree + Strongly Disagree)

What examiners look for here:

Trends
Skews (mostly positive? mixed?)
Outliers (a few low scores)

Avoid statements like:
“Most users liked it.”

Instead say:
“The majority of users rated this feature positively, with a small proportion indicating difficulty.”


Assign simple codes
Short phrases representing what the user is sayin


Group codes into themes
Example themes for your system:

Navigation clarity
Ease of completing tasks
Error handling and feedback
Producer dashboard usability
Accessibility and layout



Support themes with evidence
Use short quotes, not assumptions.
Example: Several users mentioned navigation clarity as a strength, with one stating, “I found the products quickly without needing help.”


 Identify limitations (Examiners EXPECT this)
Briefly acknowledge constraints:

Small sample size
Some yes/no questions limit depth
Testing limited to supervised sessions

Example phrasing: Due to the limited sample size, results represent patterns rather than definitive user behaviour, however they provide sufficient insight to inform future development.
